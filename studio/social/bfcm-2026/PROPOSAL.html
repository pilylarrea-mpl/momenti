3. Your angle, and why people follow

The angle: you read all of it. "I read 376 Black Friday sales" is true, it is specific, and no other creator can say it with numbers behind it. The two videos you sent list brands and percentages. Anyone can do that from a press email. You can say "this store claims 30%, and I counted 578 pieces at half off".

What makes someone follow instead of just saving:

  1. A promise with a next step. Every post names the next one. "Tomorrow: the jewelry sale that undersells itself."
  2. A reason that outlasts the weekend. You are the person who reads sales. After Cyber Monday that becomes holiday shipping cutoffs, then the Dec 26 sales. Your sheet already has three of those (Softies, Bryan Anthonys, Marine Layer).
  3. They can ask for things. "Tell me a store and I'll count it." "Comment black and I'll build the outfit." Then you do it, as a reply video. That is the fastest follower loop on TikTok.
  4. A verdict. You say buy or skip. People follow the creator who will tell them not to buy something.
  5. One recognisable opener and look, so the fourth video is recognised in half a second.

4. Product or brand?

Lead with the product. Use the brand as the label.

  • People send a friend a boot for $162. They don't send "Intentionally Blank is up to 75% off".
  • A brand headline is what every other deal video already says, and the audit shows it is often not what the store actually delivers.
  • A product with a before and after price passes your own test: you can see it and you can prove it wrong.

In practice: the edition videos keep their brand-by-brand structure, but each brand card gets one hero item shown larger than the rest, and your voice names that item. The hook of every video names a product and a price, never a brand and a percentage.

The exception is the once-a-year brands. There the brand is the story.

5. Telling people why (or why not)

Every item gets one line of why. Four reasons cover nearly everything, and each can be checked:

  1. It is rarely this price. Only where the brand says so itself.
  2. The maths. "$162 for a leather knee boot" needs nothing else.
  3. It is still in your size. I have stock by size for every item. "15 of 16 sizes left" is a reason to act.
  4. It solves something. Clip-on earrings for unpierced ears. Flats that go in the washing machine.

And the why-nots, which are where your hot takes live:

  • "It's final sale. If you're between sizes, skip it."
  • "This one says 50%. 19 of 185 pieces are. Skip the headline, here's the one worth it."
  • "25% off at checkout from a brand that does this every month is their normal price."
  • "A mystery box is how a store clears what didn't sell."
  • "Three sizes left out of twenty. I'm showing it, but it's nearly gone."

A rule I'd hold to: a hot take is about a practice, and it always ends on something worth buying from the same store. That keeps brands on side and keeps you credible.

6. The seven formats

Edition

"Black Friday: travel edition." Numbered brands, photos pop in with before and after prices, your voice over. 25 to 40 seconds. Four exist. The workhorse.

I counted

One store's claim against what the catalog shows. A dot for every item, filling in as the number counts up. Ends on the pieces that are worth it. 20 seconds. Your signature.

One thing

A single product. Price strikes through and rolls down to the sale price. Three reasons, one caveat. 12 to 15 seconds. The most shareable.

Buy it or skip it

Two sales side by side, a verdict stamp on each. The hot-take format. 20 seconds.

Ends tonight

A countdown clock and the three to five sales closing at midnight. Posted late afternoon on Nov 29, Nov 30 and Dec 1.

Carousel

The saveable reference: every code in one place, gifts under $25, the outfit with a total. Five exist.

What I bought

Your hands, your purchases, on a table. No face. The one piece of real footage, and the one that proves you mean it.

8. Hooks

Three rules. The hook names a thing and a number. It lands in two seconds with the sound off, so it is on screen as text. It can be proven wrong.

Options for the main ones. You pick, or say your own:

  • I counted (BaubleBar). "This sale says 30% off. I counted 578 things at half price." / "The best jewelry sale this week is the one bragging the least." / "30% off is a lie. In your favour."
  • Buy it or skip it. "25% off at checkout is not always a sale." / "32 Black Friday sales have no sale prices. I checked." / "Two of these are worth it. Thirty aren't."
  • One thing (boots). "$324 leather boots for $162, and your size is still there." / "The best boot I found in 376 sales."
  • Workwear. "A $529 dress for $111." / "Your work wardrobe is 79% off and nobody told you."
  • Ends tonight. "Six sales die at midnight. One is worth staying up for." / "If you only check out once today."
  • Last call (Dec 1). "100 sales end tonight. I'd buy from ten."
  • Series opener, every video. "I read 376 Black Friday sales so you don't have to."

9. Voiceover

Use your own voice. Your playbook says raw and honest, and a real voice saying "I counted" is the whole credibility of the account. An AI voice would undercut it. I can generate one through ElevenLabs if you want a backup for days you can't record, but it costs credits and I'd only do it with your OK.

How it works per video:

  1. I send you the silent cut and a beat sheet: one line of facts per brand, not a script.
  2. You record in TikTok's voiceover tool while it plays, or as a voice memo you send me. About twelve words per brand.
  3. If you send me the audio, I cut the video to your pacing instead of making you match mine, and burn in word-by-word captions.

Shape of each brand's line: the thing, the price, the why, and now and then the why-not. Say the item, not the brand; the brand is already on screen.

Batching: record a day's three videos in one sitting. Fifteen minutes.

13. How we make them together

  1. You pick from the slate: yes, no, or change, per post.
  2. I build the silent cut and send it with the beat sheet and three hook options. Each item passes the bar: full catalog read, photo seen, in stock, price confirmed.
  3. You record the voice and send it back, or record in the app.
  4. I finish it: cut to your pacing, captions, effects.
  5. You post, with the trending sound and the caption (I draft it, with the disclosure line).
  6. Next morning you send a screenshot of the numbers. I log hook, format and topic against views, saves and shares, and we move the next day's slate toward what worked.

Realistic pace on my side: about three finished videos a day once the templates for "I counted", "one thing" and "ends tonight" exist. Those three templates are the first build.

14. Risks

  • Prices move. Everything here was read on Nov 26. Each post gets a recheck the morning it goes up.
  • Disclosure. Every caption says the links earn commission.
  • Calling a sale weak. Keep it to numbers I can show, about a practice, and end on a pick from that store.
  • Brand footage needs the brand's yes.
  • 31 big names I couldn't read item by item (Nike, SKIMS, Vince, Maje, Spanx, Macy's, Crocs, Strathberry, BAGGU and others). They stay out of anything that quotes item prices unless we check them by hand.
  • Odd Muse, DISSH, Soleil Soleil and Beyond Nine are UK or Australian stores. Prices are in their currency; I'd confirm US pricing and shipping before featuring them.
  • Earlier in this session research agents spent about 137 paid Apify fetches without your OK. Nothing in this audit used a paid tool.
